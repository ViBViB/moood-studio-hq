Preoptima — Logo, round two · our response

Five questions,
before we draw anything else.

Where this stands

Nobody said the concept is wrong. Nobody said abandon it. What you have is a group that was shown a bare symbol on a white page — most of them on holiday — and asked whether they liked it.

That format has a predictable outcome, and it isn't a reflection on your team. A group asked to judge a symbol in isolation will drift toward the least objectionable option, because “not convinced” is the cheapest available answer when there's no context and no criteria to judge against.

The least objectionable option is usually the corporate one you told us at the start you specifically didn't want.

So I'd read the “unresolved middle” as information about how the question was asked, rather than as a verdict on the mark.

Five questions worth putting to the team

Deliberately narrow. None of them asks whether someone likes it — which is the question that produced the answers you're struggling with.

01

Looking at the symbol on its own — what do you see? One word, first instinct, don't think about it.

WhyThis tells us whether the mark is legible on its own, which is the thing the team asked us for last round. If the answers scatter, that's a clear and fixable brief. If they converge, the mark is working harder than the reactions suggest.

02

Setting the logo aside — does “a company that shows you where a decision leads before you commit to it” still describe Preoptima?

WhyThis separates the idea from the drawing. If it's still yes, then we're only debating execution, and that's a much shorter conversation than it currently feels like.

03

If this became the logo tomorrow, what specifically would embarrass you?

WhyThe most useful question on the list. Vague discomfort can't be designed against; a specific objection can. This is the one I'd push hardest on.

04

Which is closer to right — this mark taken further, or a different idea altogether?

WhyThis is the continue-or-pivot call in a single line, and it's the answer we need most. Either is a good outcome. Not knowing is the only bad one.

05

Last round you pointed us to Figma, Extantia and Reverion — clean, letter-derived marks that stand on their own. Do you feel this one isn't in that territory?

WhyThat reference set was the clearest steer we've had, and it's exactly what we designed against. If this mark does sit in that territory, then the hesitation is about something else, and it's worth naming. If it doesn't, we need to know precisely where it falls short.

And one for you rather than the group: it would help to know whether the strong dislike is with the concept, with this execution, or with the overall style. An unattributed strong no is the kind of thing that can quietly veto a decision later — better surfaced now.

What happens once we have answers

Both routes are short, and we'd move quickly on either.

  • If the idea holds — the next thing we'd send isn't another symbol on a white page. It's the mark in use: a business card, a hero section, a PACER screen, an avatar at real size, embroidered on a shirt. You were in the room for the thinking; the team got a link and a shape. Almost no mark is loved in isolation in black and white, and most become obvious the moment you see them doing their job.
  • If it doesn't — that's a legitimate answer and we'll take it without argument. We'd want question 03 answered properly first, so the next concept isn't built on the same guesswork.

What “enough” looks like

One thing worth saying plainly, because it may take some pressure off: you don't need the team to be unanimously enthusiastic in order to move forward.

There's a difference between using the team's input to sharpen the work and waiting for the team to feel unanimous about it. The first makes the work better. The second, in our experience, makes it safer.

What you need is enough clarity to take the next step — and a mark that earns the rest of the team over time, in use.

On timing

We're at the three-week mark we scoped at kickoff. I'm not raising that to rush anyone — but identity work doesn't improve with an open-ended decision window. It erodes.

Could we agree a date by which the answers come back, even if what they say is “still split”? Having a date is worth more than having consensus.

And thank you for being straight about where this is stuck. It's a better position to work from than a polished answer none of us believed.
