Preoptima — Identity direction · Round three

The name is the mark.

You asked for two routes: one that keeps the P, one that drops the letter. Both arrived at the same place — the strongest thing Preoptima owns is its name, and a symbol beside it was competing with it rather than adding to it. So in both, the drawing happens inside the letters.

Each holds a second idea in the word: a point where a path divides, and something to steer by before it does. You don't need to find it for the logo to work.

The last letter is where the drawing happens. Built from triangles instead of curves, it carries more than one reading: some people find a star, some a compass, some two edges turning away from one another.

None of them is required, and none of them is the point. The word is legible first. This is the more technological of the two — the register of a company whose product does the calculation.

Here the drawing happens near the end of the word. One letter leans out of line and lifts its point away from the stem: the moment a path stops running straight. Held together, some people read a small flame.

Wide-set capitals, closer to the register architects and engineers already work in. Where the first direction answers a weak identity with weight, this one answers it with precision — that is the real choice between them.
